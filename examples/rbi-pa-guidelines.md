# RBI’s Latest Guidelines for Payment Aggregators and Their Effect on UPI-First Companies

**Domain:** Regulatory  
**Report Type:** Research Report (Markdown)  
**Status:** Compiled from gathered research notes; certain provisions of the September 2025 Master Direction are available only in truncated excerpts.

---

## 1. Executive Summary

The current operative framework governing Payment Aggregators (PAs) and Payment Gateways (PGs) in India is the **Reserve Bank of India (Regulation of Payment Aggregators) Directions, 2025**, issued as **Master Direction CO.DPSS.POLC.No.S-633/02-14-008/2025-26 dated September 15, 2025** [7]. These Directions supersede the earlier circular RBI/2020-21/117 dated March 31, 2021 [1], which itself updated the original guidelines of March 17, 2020 [2].

Key takeaways from the available research:

* **Applicability:** The September 15, 2025 Master Direction applies to all bank and non-bank entities undertaking the business of a Payment Aggregator (PA) [7]. It is effective immediately unless otherwise indicated for specific provisions [7].
* **Authorization:** Entities carrying on only PA-P (online Payment Aggregator) business must apply for RBI authorization by **December 31, 2025** [7]. Entities that fail to apply by the due date must intimate their banker(s) forthwith and wind up their business by **February 28, 2026** [7].
* **Net worth:** Non-bank PA-P entities existing on the date of the circular must hold a minimum net worth of **₹15 crore at the time of application** and **₹25 crore by March 31, 2028**, to be maintained at all times thereafter [4]. New non-bank PA-P entities must have ₹15 crore at the time of application and ₹25 crore by the end of the third financial year of grant of authorization [4].
* **Nodal / Escrow accounts:** Under the superseded March 31, 2021 framework, existing entities could continue maintaining nodal accounts until authorized by RBI, with an expectation to move toward escrow accounts [1]. Specific nodal/escrow mechanics under the 2025 Master Direction are not fully reproduced in the available research excerpts.
* **UPI-first companies (TPAPs, PSP banks, UPI-based payment apps):** The available excerpts of the September 2025 Master Direction do not contain a detailed liability-allocation matrix, settlement responsibility framework, or specific grievance-disposal timelines for UPI transactions routed through PAs/PGs. Relevant UPI obligations are found in older instruments—particularly the PPI Master Direction CO.DPSS.POLC.No.S-479/02.14.006/2021-22 dated August 27, 2021 [6] and the original PA/PG guidelines of March 17, 2020 [2]—which address PPI–UPI interoperability, sponsor-bank settlement, and PA disclosure/grievance requirements.

**Research limitation:** Multiple research points explicitly note that the full text of the September 15, 2025 Master Direction is available only in truncated form. Consequently, specific provisions on deemed/interim authorization, PG transition timelines, PPI transitions under the 2025 MD, monetary penalties for PAs/PGs, RBI inspection rights, and granular UPI settlement/liability allocation are **not present** in the supplied sources. This report reflects only what is contained in the research notes and flags gaps where applicable.

### 2.2 Definitions and Entity Classification

* **Payment Aggregators (PAs):** Entities that facilitate e-commerce sites and merchants to accept various payment instruments [2].
* **Payment Gateways (PGs):** Not fully defined in the available excerpts of the 2025 Master Direction; the research notes identify a need to distinguish PAs, PGs, and PPI issuers, but the full definitional text is absent [1][7].
* **Prepaid Payment Instrument (PPI) issuers:** A separate entity class regulated under the PPI Master Direction (CO.DPSS.POLC.No.S-479/02.14.006/2021-22 dated August 27, 2021) [6].
* **Prior authorization:** Online non-bank PAs existing as on March 17, 2020 were previously required to apply for RBI authorization under the Payment and Settlement Systems Act, 2007 (PSS Act) [3]. The 2025 Master Direction requires fresh authorization applications from PA-P entities by December 31, 2025 [7]. The research notes do not explicitly state whether PGs require prior RBI authorization under the 2025 MD.

### 2.3 Authorization, Net Worth, and Capital Requirements

* **Application deadline (PA-P):** An entity carrying on only PA-P business shall apply for authorization by **December 31, 2025** [7].
* **Consequence of non-application:** An entity that fails to apply by the due date shall intimate its banker(s) forthwith and wind up its business by **February 28, 2026** [7].
* **Incomplete applications:** Applications not meeting the minimum capital requirement, or which are incomplete / not in the prescribed form, shall be returned [7].
* **Net worth — existing non-bank PA-P:** Minimum net worth of **₹15 crore at the time of submitting application** and **₹25 crore by March 31, 2028**; ₹25 crore shall be maintained at all times thereafter [4]. Example: if a Certificate of Authorization is granted on October 1, 2025, the entity shall achieve ₹25 crore net worth by March 31, 2028 [4].
* **Net worth — new non-bank PA-P:** Minimum net worth of ₹15 crore at the time of application; ₹25 crore by end of the third financial year of grant of authorization [4].
* **Historical net worth:** Under the previous regime, online non-bank PAs had to meet a minimum net worth criterion of ₹15 crore by March 31, 2021 [3].

### 2.4 Nodal / Escrow Account Maintenance

* Under the March 31, 2021 circular, existing entities could continue to maintain nodal accounts until authorized by RBI. Because the PA needed to move toward an escrow account, the bank and the PA could decide on maintaining the same from an earlier date [1].
* The research excerpts of the September 2025 Master Direction do not reproduce the detailed nodal/escrow account maintenance requirements [7].

### 2.6 Provisions Applying to UPI-First Companies

* **PPI–UPI interoperability:** Under the PPI Master Direction dated August 27, 2021 [6], holders of only full-KYC PPIs may make UPI payments by linking customer PPIs to their UPI handle. A PPI issuer, in its capacity as a PSP, shall not on-board customers of any bank or any other PPI issuer [6].
* **UPI settlement:** Non-bank PPI issuers participate in UPI settlement through a sponsor bank and must meet NPCI requirements [11].
* **General PA obligations (from March 17, 2020 guidelines):** Agreements between PAs, merchants, acquiring banks, and stakeholders must delineate roles and responsibilities for complaints, refunds/failed transactions, return policy, customer grievance redressal (including turnaround time), dispute resolution, and reconciliation [2]. PAs must disclose comprehensive merchant policies, customer grievance details, privacy policy, and other terms [2].
* **2025 Master Direction and UPI-first entities:** The available truncated excerpts do not specify distinct compliance obligations for TPAPs, UPI PSP entities, or UPI-based payment apps under the September 2025 Master Direction, including settlement responsibility, liability allocation among PA/PG–PSP bank–NPCI, or transaction data storage/reporting timelines specific to UPI [7].

## 3. Detailed Analysis

### 3.1 Transition from the 2021 Framework to the 2025 Master Direction

The Reserve Bank replaced the March 31, 2021 PA/PG circular with the Master Direction dated September 15, 2025 [1][7]. The new instrument applies immediately to all bank and non-bank PA entities [7]. While the previous circular required existing PAs to apply by September 30, 2021 and maintain ₹15 crore net worth by March 31, 2021 [3], the 2025 Master Direction establishes a new application window closing on **December 31, 2025** for PA-only entities [7], with a mandatory wind-up date of **February 28, 2026** for non-applicants [7].

### 3.2 Capital and Authorization Regime

The research confirms a tiered net-worth structure for non-bank PA-P entities [4]:

* **At application:** ₹15 crore (both existing and new non-bank PA-Ps).
* **By March 31, 2028:** ₹25 crore for existing non-bank PA-Ps, maintained at all times thereafter.
* **By end of third financial year of authorization:** ₹25 crore for new non-bank PA-Ps.

Applications that are incomplete or fail to meet minimum capital requirements will be returned [7]. The notes do not state whether entities operating under the 2021 framework receive deemed or interim authorization under the 2025 Master Direction, nor the validity period of any such interim status [7].

### 3.3 UPI-First Business Models: Coverage and Gaps

UPI-first companies—such as UPI-based payment apps, Third-Party Application Providers (TPAPs), and UPI PSP entities—sit at the intersection of PA/PG regulation, PPI regulation, and NPCI/UPI operating rules. The research notes indicate:

1. **PPI issuers in UPI:** Full-KYC PPIs can be linked to UPI handles; PPI issuers acting as PSPs cannot on-board customers of other banks or PPI issuers [6].
2. **Settlement:** Non-bank PPI issuers settle UPI transactions through sponsor banks and must comply with NPCI requirements [11].
3. **PA-level obligations:** PAs must ensure contracts clearly allocate responsibility for grievance redressal, refunds, failed transactions, and dispute resolution [2].

However, the research notes **do not contain** the specific provisions of the September 2025 Master Direction that address:
* Whether a UPI-first company is classified as a PA, PG, or neither under the 2025 MD.
* Liability allocation for failed UPI transactions among PA/PG, PSP bank, and NPCI.
* Prescribed timelines for grievance disposal and refunds for UPI transactions routed via PAs/PGs.
* Data storage and reporting obligations unique to UPI-centric models under the 2025 MD.

### 3.4 Merchant Onboarding, Liability, and Grievance Redressal

From the March 17, 2020 guidelines (still referenced as context [2]):

* Merchant onboarding norms require agreements between PAs, merchants, acquiring banks, and all stakeholders to delineate roles in handling complaints, refunds, failed transactions, return policies, grievance redressal turnaround times, dispute resolution, and reconciliation [2].
* PAs must disclose comprehensive information on merchant policies, customer grievances, privacy policies, and terms [2].

The September 2025 Master Direction’s specific provisions on these topics are not reproduced in the available excerpts [7].

### 3.5 Enforcement, Reporting, and Supervision

Available reporting obligations from related RBI frameworks include:

* **PPI issuers** must submit: Net-worth Certificate, Declaration and Undertaking by Director, PPI Statistics, Auditor Certificate on maintenance of balance in Escrow Account, and PPI Customer Grievance Report [13].
* **Reconciliation:** PPI issuers must reconcile positions daily, weekly, monthly, or more frequently as applicable, and adhere to dispute resolution/grievance redressal mechanisms prescribed by card networks/UPI/NPCI [11].

The research notes **do not establish** specific monetary penalties, regulatory actions, or RBI inspection/audit rights for PAs and PGs under the September 2025 Master Direction [5][13].
